# Filled-in examples

Three finished `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` pairs, one for each template in `templates/`, plus one
annotated file that names every section and says why it is there.

Every business, person, price and handle in these files is invented. They exist so you can see
what "done" looks like before you fill in your own.

| Folder | Invented business | Matches template |
|---|---|---|
| `freelancer/` | Quill and Comma Copy, a one-person email copywriter | `templates/freelancer` |
| `personal-brand/` | Tin Pan Budget, a budget cooking creator | `templates/personal-brand` |
| `small-business/` | Ridgeline Landscaping, a two-crew landscaping company | `templates/small-business` |
| `annotated/` | Ridgeline Landscaping again, with a label and a reason on every section | none, read it first |

## How to use them

1. Read `annotated/AGENTS-annotated.md` first. It takes two minutes.
2. Open the example closest to your work beside your own `AGENTS.md` and compare section by section.
3. Do not copy an example into your folder. Its facts are not your facts. Copy a template from
   `templates/` and fill it in, or ask Claude to interview you (the fill-the-template prompt).

## What every example shows

- `CLAUDE.md` is short on purpose. Line 1 is `@AGENTS.md`, which pulls the facts in. Open
  `AGENTS.md` to read or change your instructions.
- When Claude updates your rules, the change lands in `AGENTS.md`, not `CLAUDE.md`.
- Each `AGENTS.md` is under 120 lines. Aim for under 120 lines, never over 200.
- Each hard rule carries its reason. A rule with its why is followed better.
- No placeholder is left. Search your own file for `<` before you rely on it.
